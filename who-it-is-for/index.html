Who it is for

For people who have to stand behind the result

An accessibility report is only useful if the person signing it can show how each finding was reached, and say what was not tested.

Different jobs, one audit

Who gets the most from it

Agencies and freelance auditors

Do the repeatable part of a client audit in minutes, with a screenshot for every finding, and spend your own time on the criteria that need judgment. The checklist shows which those are.

QA and engineering teams

Run the same audit on a developer's machine and in the build pipeline. A baseline accepts today's known issues, so a build fails only when something new appears.

Organizations with accessibility duties

The European Accessibility Act has applied since June 2025, and public-sector rules apply in many countries. The app gives you a dated record of what was tested and what was found. It does not replace a full audit by a person.

Teams that cannot upload their pages

Banking, healthcare and public-sector teams often cannot send a staging site or a logged-in page to an online service. Here nothing leaves the computer the audit runs on.

Before you ask for a trial

Is it a fit?

It is a good fit if you audit websites or web applications that run in a browser, and you want findings you can verify one by one.

It is not the right tool for native mobile apps or PDF documents, and it will not fix a site for you.
